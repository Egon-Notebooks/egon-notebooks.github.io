- Acceptance and commitment therapy — often referred to as ACT — is a modern, evidence-based approach to mental health that helps people build a more flexible relationship with their thoughts and feelings. Rather than trying to eliminate difficult emotions, ACT encourages people to make room for them while still moving toward what matters most in their lives.
- One of the core ideas in ACT is that struggling to push away painful thoughts can sometimes make them feel more overwhelming. Many people find it helpful to practice simply noticing a thought — recognizing it without immediately judging it or treating it as an absolute truth. This skill, sometimes called cognitive defusion, can gradually reduce the power that distressing thoughts have over everyday decisions and actions.
- Another important element of ACT is the concept of values — identifying what genuinely matters to you, whether that is connection, creativity, honesty, or something else entirely. When people have a clearer sense of their values, they can make small, meaningful choices that feel aligned with who they want to be, even during difficult periods. This sense of purpose can serve as a steady anchor when emotions feel unpredictable.
- Mindfulness is also woven throughout ACT. Many people find that practices like gentle breathing exercises or simply paying attention to the present moment — without judgment — can help create a little space between a feeling and a reaction to it. Over time, this kind of awareness may support greater emotional [resilience]([[Building resilience]]).
- ACT is not about achieving a permanently positive mindset or eliminating struggle from life. It is about developing the inner flexibility to live meaningfully alongside the full range of human experience. If you think this approach might be helpful for you, speaking with a mental health professional can be a worthwhile first step.
