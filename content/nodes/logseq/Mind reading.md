- Mind reading — the habit of assuming we know what others are thinking — is one of the most common thinking patterns that can quietly affect our emotional wellbeing. Most of us do it at some point: convincing ourselves that a friend is annoyed, that a colleague disapproves, or that a loved one is disappointed, often without any real evidence. While this tendency is a normal part of how the human mind works, it can become a source of significant distress when it goes unchecked.
- One strategy that many people find helpful is pausing to examine the evidence. When a thought like "they must think I'm incompetent" arises, it can help to gently ask: what do I actually know? Is there concrete information supporting this, or am I filling in the blanks with [fear]([[Fear]]) or self-doubt? This kind of gentle self-questioning, often used in [cognitive behavioral therapy]([[Cognitive behavioral therapy]]) (CBT), can create a little distance between a thought and an automatic emotional reaction.
- Another approach is practicing open communication. Rather than acting on an assumption, some people find it useful to check in directly — asking a simple, calm question can often clear up a misunderstanding that might otherwise have grown into something much larger. This isn't always easy, but many people find it brings relief and strengthens connection rather than creating conflict.
- Journaling is also something many people find valuable. Writing out an assumed thought alongside what is actually known can make it easier to notice patterns in how and when mind reading tends to show up — such as during periods of [stress]([[Understanding stress]]), [low mood]([[Understanding low mood]]), or in certain relationships.
- If mind reading is contributing to [anxiety]([[Understanding anxiety]]), strained relationships, or low [self-esteem]([[Self-esteem and where it comes from]]), it may be worth talking with a mental health professional. These patterns are very common, they are understandable, and with the right support, they can change.
