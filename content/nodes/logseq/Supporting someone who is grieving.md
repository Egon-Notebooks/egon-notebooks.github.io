- [Grief]([[Understanding grief]]) is one of the most profound human experiences, and knowing how to show up for someone who is grieving can feel uncertain or even overwhelming. There is no single right way to support a grieving person, but small, consistent acts of care can make a meaningful difference during one of life's most difficult times.
- One of the most valuable things you can offer is simply your presence. Many people feel pressure to say the "right thing," but grief often asks less of us than we think. Sitting with someone, listening without trying to fix or reframe their pain, and letting them talk — or not talk — at their own pace can feel deeply comforting. Many people who have experienced loss share that having someone witness their grief, without judgment or the urge to rush them through it, was what helped most.
- It also helps to offer practical, specific support rather than open-ended offers. Saying "I'm here if you need anything" is kind, but grief can make it hard to ask for help. Reaching out with something concrete — dropping off a meal, helping with errands, or checking in with a brief message weeks after the loss — takes the burden of asking off the grieving person's shoulders. Grief doesn't follow a timeline, and ongoing support in the weeks and months that follow can be just as meaningful as the support offered immediately after a loss.
- Finally, it's worth remembering that grief looks different for everyone. Allowing someone to grieve in their own way, without comparing their experience to others or suggesting they should feel differently, honors their individual process.
- If you are supporting someone whose grief feels overwhelming — for them or for you — professional support is available and can help both the person grieving and those who care for them.
