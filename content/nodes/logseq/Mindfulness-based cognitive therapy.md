- Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy, often referred to as MBCT, is an approach that combines traditional cognitive therapy techniques with mindfulness practices. It was originally developed to help people who experience recurrent depression, though research suggests it may be helpful for a range of mental health challenges. At its core, MBCT teaches people to relate differently to their thoughts and feelings — not by pushing them away, but by observing them with curiosity and without judgment.
- One of the central ideas in MBCT is learning to notice when the mind begins to spiral into unhelpful thought patterns. Many people who experience depression or [anxiety]([[Understanding anxiety]]) find that certain thinking habits — such as ruminating on the past or [catastrophizing]([[Catastrophizing]]) about the future — can deepen distress. MBCT helps build awareness of these patterns as they arise, which can create a small but meaningful pause between a thought and a reaction. Over time, many people find that this awareness makes those patterns feel less automatic and overwhelming.
- Practical elements of MBCT typically include guided meditation, body scan exercises, and breathing practices, alongside structured reflection on thoughts and emotions. These are usually introduced gradually within a group or individual setting, often over an eight-week program. Some people also find that short, informal mindfulness moments woven into daily life — such as bringing full attention to a simple routine task — can help reinforce this way of being present.
- It is completely natural to feel uncertain about trying a new approach, and not every strategy works equally well for every person. If you are curious about MBCT, speaking with a mental health professional can help you explore whether it might be a good fit for your situation. Many people find that having professional guidance makes it easier to engage with these practices in a meaningful and supportive way.
