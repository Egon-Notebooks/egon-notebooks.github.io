- Somatic therapy is an approach to mental health care that recognizes the deep connection between the mind and body. Rather than focusing solely on thoughts and feelings, somatic therapy invites people to pay attention to physical sensations — such as tension, warmth, or tightness — as a way of understanding and processing emotional experiences. For many people, this body-aware approach opens up new pathways to healing, particularly when words alone feel insufficient.
- One of the core ideas behind somatic therapy is that [stress]([[Understanding stress]]), [trauma]([[Understanding trauma]]), and difficult emotions can become stored in the body over time. Many people notice this without fully recognizing it — a clenched jaw during a stressful week, shallow breathing in moments of [anxiety]([[Understanding anxiety]]), or shoulders that rarely seem to relax. Somatic therapy helps individuals develop greater awareness of these patterns and gently explore what they might mean, often in collaboration with a trained therapist.
- Some strategies that may support this kind of body-mind awareness include grounding exercises, which involve consciously noticing physical contact with the floor or chair to feel more present; slow, intentional breathing practices; and gentle movement or body scanning, where a person mentally moves their attention from one part of the body to another to notice areas of tension or ease. These techniques are often used within a therapeutic relationship but can also be explored independently as part of a broader self-care routine.
- It is worth noting that somatic approaches are not a replacement for other forms of mental health support — rather, they can complement talking therapies, medication, or other strategies a person may already be using. If you are curious about whether somatic therapy might be a good fit for you, speaking with a mental health professional is a helpful first step. Many people find that working with the body as well as the mind brings a greater sense of wholeness and wellbeing.
