- Personalization is a common thinking pattern in which a person takes excessive personal responsibility for events or situations that are not entirely — or not at all — within their control. It might sound like an internal voice saying "that happened because of me" or "if I had done something differently, everything would be fine." While a sense of personal responsibility is healthy, personalization can quietly erode [self-esteem]([[Self-esteem and where it comes from]]) and contribute to feelings of guilt, [shame]([[Shame and guilt]]), and [anxiety]([[Understanding anxiety]]).
- One strategy many people find helpful is learning to notice when personalization is happening. Because this thinking pattern tends to feel automatic and even logical in the moment, simply pausing and asking "what else might have contributed to this situation?" can begin to loosen its grip. Most events involve multiple factors — other people's choices, circumstances, timing — and gently widening that view can bring some relief.
- Another approach involves practicing what therapists sometimes call "decatastrophizing" alongside personalization work: recognizing not only that an outcome wasn't solely your fault, but also that the outcome itself may be more manageable than it feels in a difficult moment. Journaling about a situation from a neutral observer's perspective — imagining what you might say to a friend in the same position — is something many people find useful for gaining that distance.
- It may also help to explore where personalization patterns began. For some people, this habit of thought developed as a way of making sense of difficult experiences early in life. Understanding its roots, often with the support of a therapist, can make it feel less like a character flaw and more like a learned response — one that can be gently unlearned over time.
- If personalization is affecting your wellbeing, reaching out to a mental health professional is a meaningful step. With the right support, it is possible to develop a more balanced and compassionate relationship with yourself.
