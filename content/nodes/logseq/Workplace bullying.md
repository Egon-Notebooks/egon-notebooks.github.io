- Workplace bullying is more common than many people realize, and its effects can extend well beyond the office. Repeated mistreatment at work — such as being excluded, undermined, humiliated, or subjected to persistent criticism — can take a real toll on a person's emotional wellbeing, confidence, and sense of safety. Recognizing that what you are experiencing is bullying, and that it is not your fault, is an important first step.
- One strategy that many people find helpful is documenting incidents as they occur. Keeping a private record of dates, times, what was said or done, and who was present can help clarify patterns and provide useful information if you decide to report the behavior formally. It can also help you feel less alone with the experience, since the act of writing things down can make it easier to process difficult emotions.
- Reaching out for support is another meaningful step. Talking with a trusted colleague, a friend outside of work, or a mental health professional can help reduce the isolation that workplace bullying often creates. Some people find that speaking with a manager, HR representative, or union contact — particularly if they feel safe doing so — opens pathways to change within the workplace. Knowing your organization's policies around workplace conduct can also help you understand your options.
- Looking after your mental and physical health during this time matters, too. Many people find that maintaining routines, getting enough rest, and setting clear [boundaries]([[Setting healthy boundaries]]) between work and personal time can help protect their overall wellbeing while they navigate a difficult situation.
- It is important to know that support is available, and that many people who have experienced workplace bullying have gone on to find healthier, more fulfilling work environments. You do not have to manage this alone, and reaching out — to someone you trust or to a professional — can make a real difference.
