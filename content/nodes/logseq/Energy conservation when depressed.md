- _If you are having a very hard time or thinking about harming yourself, you don't have to face it alone. Crisis lines in your country are listed at [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com). If you are in immediate danger, call your local emergency number._
- Everyday tasks can feel surprisingly heavy when someone is experiencing depression. Getting out of bed, preparing a meal, or responding to a message may require an amount of effort that feels completely out of proportion — and that experience is real, not a sign of weakness or laziness. Understanding how to work with limited energy, rather than against it, can make a meaningful difference on difficult days.
- One approach many people find helpful is sometimes called "pacing." Rather than pushing through a full to-do list and then crashing, pacing involves breaking tasks into smaller steps and spreading them across the day or week. For example, instead of cleaning an entire kitchen, simply washing a few dishes counts as a genuine accomplishment. Honoring small completions can gently rebuild a sense of capability over time.
- Another strategy is prioritizing by necessity and nourishment. When energy is scarce, it can help to identify which tasks genuinely need to happen — such as eating, hydrating, or taking medication — and to treat rest as a legitimate and productive activity rather than something to feel guilty about. Sleep, gentle movement, and time spent in natural light are areas where many people find even modest effort can support their overall wellbeing.
- It also helps to be intentional about where social and emotional energy goes. Some interactions feel draining while others feel sustaining. When possible, spending even brief time with people or activities that feel supportive rather than depleting can protect a person's limited reserves.
- Depression is not a permanent state, and many people find that their capacity gradually returns as they receive support and develop strategies that work for them. If low energy and [low mood]([[Understanding low mood]]) have been persisting for a while, speaking with a healthcare or mental health professional is a worthwhile step — help is available, and things can improve.
