- Overgeneralization is a common thinking pattern that can quietly shape the way we see ourselves and the world around us. It happens when one difficult experience gets stretched into a sweeping conclusion — for example, making a mistake at work and then deciding "I always mess things up." While this kind of thinking is very human, it can become a heavy weight to carry when it goes unchecked.
- One way many people find helpful is simply noticing when overgeneralized thoughts appear. Words like "always," "never," "everyone," and "nothing ever" can be useful signals. When these words show up in a thought, it may be worth pausing and asking: is this actually true all of the time? Often, the honest answer is no — and that small moment of questioning can loosen the thought's grip.
- Another approach involves looking for exceptions. If a part of you is insisting "I never do anything right," gently searching for even one example where something did go right can begin to challenge that belief. This isn't about forcing positivity — it's about building a more accurate and balanced picture of reality. Over time, practicing this kind of gentle examination can help shift deeply ingrained patterns.
- Some people also find it useful to try writing their thoughts down. Seeing a thought on paper can create a little distance from it, making it easier to examine with curiosity rather than self-criticism. [[Cognitive behavioral therapy]], often called CBT, is one well-researched approach that works directly with patterns like overgeneralization, and many people have found it genuinely helpful.
- If overgeneralized thinking feels persistent or is affecting your mood and daily life, reaching out for support is a meaningful and courageous step. These patterns can change — and no one has to work through them alone.
