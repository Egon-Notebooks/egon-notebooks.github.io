- [Stress]([[Understanding stress]]) and tension have a way of settling into the body without us noticing — a tight jaw, hunched shoulders, or a knot in the stomach. The body scan is a simple mindfulness practice that invites people to slow down and gently pay attention to physical sensations from head to toe. It can be a helpful tool for reconnecting with the body and building greater awareness of how emotions and stress show up physically.
- The practice itself is straightforward. A person typically lies down or sits comfortably, closes their eyes, and slowly moves their attention through different parts of the body — starting at the feet and gradually working upward, or the reverse. The goal is not to change or fix anything, but simply to notice. Some people observe warmth, tension, tingling, or numbness; others find that certain areas feel neutral. There is no right or wrong way to experience it.
- Research suggests that regular body scan practice may support stress reduction and emotional regulation. It is a core component of Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR), a structured program developed to help people manage chronic stress, pain, and difficult emotions. Many people find that practicing even a short version — five to ten minutes — can help create a sense of calm and groundedness throughout the day.
- For those who find it difficult to stay present in their thoughts, working through the body can offer a gentler entry point into mindfulness. Some people find the practice easier with a guided audio recording, which can be found through apps, videos, or mindfulness programs.
- Like any skill, body scanning tends to become more comfortable and effective with regular practice. If you are curious about mindfulness as part of your mental health journey, speaking with a mental health professional can help you find an approach that feels right for you.
