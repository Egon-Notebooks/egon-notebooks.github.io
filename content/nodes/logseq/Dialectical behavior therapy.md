- Dialectical behavior therapy, often referred to as DBT, is a type of structured psychological treatment that helps people develop skills for managing intense emotions, improving relationships, and building a life that feels more balanced and meaningful. Originally developed to support people experiencing severe emotional distress, DBT is now used to address a wide range of challenges, including [anxiety]([[Understanding anxiety]]), depression, and difficulties with emotional regulation.
- One of the core ideas in DBT is that two things can be true at once — that a person is doing the best they can, and that they can also work toward change. This balance between acceptance and change is woven throughout the approach. Many people find this framing genuinely relieving, because it removes the pressure to simply "think differently" and instead offers concrete, learnable tools.
- DBT is typically organized around four skill areas. Mindfulness helps people observe their thoughts and feelings without immediately reacting to them. Distress tolerance offers strategies for getting through difficult moments without making a situation worse. Emotion regulation teaches ways to understand and gently influence emotional responses over time. Finally, interpersonal effectiveness focuses on communicating needs clearly and maintaining healthy relationships. These skills are often practiced in both individual therapy sessions and group settings, reinforcing them through repetition and real-world application.
- If you or someone you care about is struggling with emotional intensity or finding it hard to cope with daily life, reaching out to a mental health professional is a meaningful step. DBT has a strong evidence base and has helped many people move toward greater stability and self-understanding. Recovery and growth are possible, and no one has to navigate these challenges alone. A qualified therapist can help determine whether DBT or another approach might be a good fit.
