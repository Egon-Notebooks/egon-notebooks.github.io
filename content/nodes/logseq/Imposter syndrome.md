- Imposter syndrome is a common experience in which a person doubts their own accomplishments and fears being exposed as less capable or knowledgeable than others believe them to be. Despite evidence of their skills and achievements, they may feel like a fraud — as though their success has been a matter of luck rather than genuine ability. This experience is far more widespread than many people realize, affecting people across all kinds of professions, backgrounds, and life stages.
- One strategy that many people find helpful is recognizing and naming the pattern when it arises. Simply noticing the thought — "this is imposter syndrome talking" — can create a small but meaningful distance between the feeling and a person's sense of self. Keeping a record of accomplishments, positive feedback, and moments of genuine competence can also provide a grounding counterpoint when self-doubt feels overwhelming.
- Another approach involves reframing the relationship with uncertainty. Many people experiencing imposter syndrome hold themselves to a standard of needing to know everything, interpreting any gap in knowledge as proof that they don't belong. Learning to view uncertainty as a natural part of growth — something that skilled, experienced people also navigate — can gently shift that internal narrative. Sharing these feelings with trusted peers or mentors can also be powerful, as many people are surprised to discover how widely this experience is shared.
- It is worth remembering that imposter syndrome, while uncomfortable, does not reflect reality. Feelings of self-doubt are not accurate measures of a person's worth, capability, or right to be where they are. For those who find that these feelings are persistent or are significantly affecting their wellbeing, speaking with a mental health professional can offer meaningful support. With the right tools and guidance, many people find that their relationship with self-doubt can genuinely change over time.
