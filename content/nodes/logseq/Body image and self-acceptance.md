- Feeling comfortable in your own skin is something many people struggle with, and those feelings can shift from day to day. Body image — the way we perceive, think about, and feel toward our own bodies — is shaped by a wide range of influences, including media, personal history, and the messages we receive from the people around us. A negative body image can affect mood, [self-esteem]([[Self-esteem and where it comes from]]), and overall wellbeing, but it is not a fixed state. With time and the right support, many people find that their relationship with their body can genuinely change.
- One approach that many people find helpful is practicing mindful awareness of self-critical thoughts. Rather than accepting negative thoughts about appearance as facts, it can be useful to notice them with curiosity and gently challenge where they come from. This is a core idea in cognitive behavioral approaches, which encourage people to examine the connection between their thoughts, feelings, and behaviors without judgment.
- Another strategy involves shifting focus toward what the body can do rather than how it looks. Engaging in movement that feels enjoyable rather than punishing, paying attention to physical sensations of comfort, rest, and nourishment — these small redirections can gradually build a more respectful inner relationship with the body. Some people also find that limiting time on social media, particularly content that promotes narrow beauty ideals, creates meaningful breathing room for self-acceptance to grow.
- Journaling, creative expression, and connecting with communities that celebrate body diversity are other avenues that many people find supportive along the way.
- It is worth remembering that struggling with body image is incredibly common, and you do not have to navigate it alone. Speaking with a mental health professional can offer personalized guidance and a safe space to explore these feelings more deeply. Reaching out is a sign of self-awareness, and that is always a meaningful first step.
