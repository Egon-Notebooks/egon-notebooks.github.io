- Bullying is a painful experience that can affect people of all ages, and its impact often extends well beyond the moments when it occurs. Understanding how bullying can affect mental health is an important step toward recognizing these experiences and finding a path forward.
- One of the most common effects of bullying is a heightened sense of [anxiety]([[Understanding anxiety]]) or [fear]([[Fear]]). People who have been bullied may find themselves feeling on edge in social situations, anticipating harm or rejection even in environments where they are safe. Over time, this can make everyday interactions feel exhausting or overwhelming. Many people find that gradually re-engaging with trusted social connections — at their own pace — can help rebuild a sense of safety and belonging.
- Bullying can also affect a person's [self-esteem]([[Self-esteem and where it comes from]]) and overall sense of worth. Repeated negative messages, whether spoken aloud or communicated through exclusion, can be internalized in ways that feel deeply personal. It's worth noting that these messages reflect the behavior of those doing the bullying, not the value of the person being targeted. Some strategies that may help include journaling, creative expression, and spending time with people who offer genuine support and encouragement.
- Feelings of [sadness]([[Sadness]]), [loneliness]([[Loneliness and connection]]), or [low mood]([[Understanding low mood]]) are also commonly experienced by people who have faced bullying. These feelings are a natural response to a genuinely difficult situation, and they do not define a person's future or their capacity for connection and [joy]([[Joy and positive emotions]]).
- Whatever someone is carrying as a result of bullying, they don't have to carry it alone. Speaking with a trusted friend, family member, or mental health professional can make a meaningful difference. Healing is possible, and many people find that with the right support, they are able to rebuild their confidence and move forward with a renewed sense of themselves.
