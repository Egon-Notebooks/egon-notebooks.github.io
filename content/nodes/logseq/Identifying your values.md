- Knowing what truly matters to you can be a quiet but powerful anchor in everyday life. Values are the personal principles that guide how we want to live — things like honesty, connection, creativity, or fairness. When our daily actions feel out of step with what we care about most, it can contribute to feelings of restlessness, [low mood]([[Understanding low mood]]), or a sense that something is missing. Taking time to identify your values is one way to build a stronger foundation for your overall wellbeing.
- One place to start is simply noticing what stirs a sense of meaning or satisfaction for you. Many people find it helpful to reflect on moments when they felt most like themselves — times when they felt engaged, proud, or at ease. These moments often point toward underlying values. Journaling about them, even briefly, can help patterns emerge over time.
- It can also be useful to notice where frustration or discomfort shows up. When something feels consistently wrong or unfair, that feeling is often a signal that a value is being compromised. For example, someone who feels drained by constant conflict may be recognizing a deep value around peace or respect. These emotional signals, rather than being obstacles, can serve as useful information.
- Once you have a clearer sense of your values, you can begin to consider small, concrete ways to honor them in your daily life. This does not mean overhauling everything at once — even minor shifts in how you spend your time or energy can create a greater sense of alignment and purpose. Many people find that this process unfolds gradually, and that is entirely okay.
- If you find it difficult to connect with your values, or feel disconnected from a sense of purpose more broadly, speaking with a mental health professional can offer meaningful support. You do not have to figure it out alone.
