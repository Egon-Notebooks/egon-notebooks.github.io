- Watching someone you care about experience bullying can feel frustrating and helpless. Whether it's happening to a child, a teenager, or an adult in a workplace setting, bullying can have a real impact on a person's emotional wellbeing, confidence, and sense of safety. Knowing how to offer meaningful support can make a significant difference to someone going through this.
- One of the most important things you can do is listen without judgment. Many people who are bullied feel ashamed or worry they won't be believed, so simply creating a safe space for them to share what's happening — without minimizing it or immediately jumping to solutions — can help them feel less alone. Phrases like "I believe you" and "I'm glad you told me" can go a long way.
- It also helps to gently encourage the person to maintain connections with people they trust. Bullying often works by isolating its target, and staying socially connected can be a protective factor for emotional [resilience]([[Building resilience]]). At the same time, it's important to follow the person's lead — pushing too hard for social activity when someone is struggling can feel overwhelming rather than supportive.
- When appropriate, it may be worth exploring practical steps together, such as documenting incidents, understanding what reporting options exist in their school or workplace, or looking into what policies are in place to address the behavior. Doing this collaboratively, rather than taking over, helps the person feel empowered rather than further disempowered.
- It's worth remembering that the effects of bullying can sometimes run deeper than they appear on the surface. If the person seems to be struggling significantly with their mood, withdrawal, or sense of self-worth, encouraging them to speak with a mental health professional is a kind and caring step. No one has to navigate these experiences alone, and support is available.
