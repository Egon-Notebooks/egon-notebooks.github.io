# Schema therapy

Schema therapy is a form of psychotherapy that helps people identify and shift deeply held patterns of thinking, feeling, and behaving that may have developed early in life. These patterns — called schemas — often form in childhood as ways of making sense of difficult experiences, but they can follow a person into adulthood and get in the way of healthy relationships, [[Self-esteem and where it comes from|self-esteem]], and overall wellbeing. Understanding where these patterns come from can be a meaningful first step toward lasting change.

One of the core ideas in schema therapy is that many emotional struggles in adulthood are connected to unmet needs from earlier in life — things like safety, connection, or validation. A therapist trained in this approach works collaboratively with a person to gently explore these patterns, helping them recognize how schemas might be influencing their thoughts and choices today. This process moves at a pace that feels manageable, with an emphasis on building trust and [[Self-compassion|self-compassion]] along the way.

Schema therapy also uses a range of practical tools to support this process. These may include guided imagery, which helps people access emotions connected to past experiences, and techniques that encourage a person to practice responding to situations in new, healthier ways. Many people find that working with both thoughts and emotions — rather than thoughts alone — helps them connect more deeply with why certain patterns feel so persistent. Over time, this can support meaningful shifts in how a person relates to themselves and others.

It is worth knowing that schemas, however long-standing, are not fixed. With the right support, people can develop greater awareness of their patterns and build more flexible, fulfilling ways of living. If you feel that old patterns may be affecting your life, speaking with a mental health professional can be a valuable place to start.
